# Countries States Cities Database v6.3.0

v6.3.0 is a minor release under data contract 6, the same contract as v6.0.0, v6.1.0 and v6.2.0. Compared with the
newest v6.2.0 download, it adds 892 city rows and changes 476 existing rows, with no id removed, moved to
another table or renumbered, and no change to the documented settlement filter. Every stored id stays valid.

Counts compare the newest download,
[v6.2.0-export.1](https://github.com/dr5hn/countries-states-cities-database/releases/tag/v6.2.0-export.1)
(source [`b6509aeb`](https://github.com/dr5hn/countries-states-cities-database/tree/b6509aebd1bf26e7b0fb4366498fadb5bb47a9ec)),
with this release's source, tag [`v6.3.0`](https://github.com/dr5hn/countries-states-cities-database/tree/v6.3.0), whose data is that of
[`3f8e8be7`](https://github.com/dr5hn/countries-states-cities-database/tree/3f8e8be78aa4a36016479e3fd3566355e58215fa).

The 44 commits from the v6.2.0 tag ([`89ce3d3c`](https://github.com/dr5hn/countries-states-cities-database/tree/89ce3d3c7aadc59ba216ba5505b202027e22e544)) to
`b6509aeb` shipped early in v6.2.0-export.1: US township items; Chinese, Indian and Philippine items;
French and Italian communes and Montevideo barrios; Pescaria Brava and Boa Esperança do Norte in Brazil;
Brazil's population estimates and time zones; Italian, Mexican, Spanish, Australian, British, Polish, Romanian
and US populations; classifications and parent links; Puerto Rico reconciliation; wrong-item corrections and
clears; the French migration supplement; contributor id guidance; and cleaned ledger receipts.

## This release is not marked "latest"

Like v6.0.0, v6.1.0 and v6.2.0, v6.3.0 and its export are published without the **latest** flag. Anything that downloads
from `releases/latest`, including the `countrystatecity` npm and PyPI packages, the export tool and the website's
download links, keeps receiving v5.2.0-export.1 for now. Download v6.3.0 by its tag.

## Structure

- Spain: 33 districts and neighbourhoods become `section`, with their own local term and verified parent. Districts
  are level 4; neighbourhoods are level 5 under a district, or level 4 under the city where the documented urban
  containment model applies. Centro keeps its id, and Las Tablas is a recognised functional barrio.
- Brazil: proved seat rows become municípios only under the reviewed seat rule, with the município's own identity.
  Smaller same-name places retain their own rows. Rio's bairros and the Federal District regions use the model above.
- Italy: 75 rows become `locality / ex comune / 4` and 43 `locality / frazione / 4`, alongside census-centre and nucleus
  classifications. An `ex comune` is the former municipal territory, not its seat; it never takes a census-centre
  count. A frazione is an inhabited subdivision and need not equal a census locality. Verified successor or containing
  comune links are filled. This work, and the earlier current-comune and census-place typing, was already in the download.
- Mexico: locality and municipio classes follow their own INEGI unit. Tototlán is the municipio; Juan Martín is an
  urban locality. These corrections were already in the download.
- Australia and Britain: council territories, suburbs, localities, parishes and settlements get their proved local
  classes and levels. A council's city status does not make it the town. The work was already in the download.
- France: current communes, communes déléguées, inhabited places and quartiers get their own classes and verified
  commune parents. Romania gains 14 village parents and 8 seat-locality links; the latter use
  `locality / localitate componentă / 3`. These changes were already in the download.
- China: Hainan gains 10 parent links and 3 level corrections, already in the download. Guizhou gains or corrects
  50 parent links and 80 levels, plus official Chinese names on two prefectures, after the download. Unproved legacy
  names and duplicate candidates stay held.

## Wikidata items

The download already carried 13,314 newly proved US township and civil-town items, matched through their own GNIS
or FIPS code, class, state and Census boundary. It also carried 31 Chinese, 7 Indian and 7 Philippine item fills
through their own official codes, and smaller corrections in France, Italy, Mexico, Australia, Romania and São Tomé.

The broad cleanup clears 360 items for non-places or distant places in another country and corrects 30; a follow-up
clears 25 more foreign namesakes. Puerto Rico reconciliation clears a river item on a city row and 25 wrong items
on municipio states. All of that was already in the download. A blank item is retained when no replacement is proved.

After the download, 213 Brazilian municípios gain or correct their own item, proved by their IBGE code, name, state,
contained stored point and unique ownership. Anaurilândia is resolved as that município, with its own item rather than
the redirect to Anastácio; its old population remains undated. The recall audit can then count an official seat town
against its proved município row without moving the stored point: the seat-rule score is 98.49%, while the strict
point-to-point score is 55.55%. This recall rule does not change the settlement filter or turn a territory into a seat.
Spain also corrects wrong municipio items, including ancient Abdera and Tarraco and the generic item for a cabin.

## Settlement filter

The rules in [TYPE_FIELD.md](https://github.com/dr5hn/countries-states-cities-database/blob/3f8e8be78aa4a36016479e3fd3566355e58215fa/TYPE_FIELD.md#filtering-to-genuine-settlements)
are unchanged. Running the documented SQL on each source gives:

| Starting snapshot | Before | After | Joined | Left |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v6.2.0-export.1 | 151,489 | 152,381 | 892 | 0 |
| v6.2.0 tag | 151,423 | 152,381 | 971 | 13 |

All 892 rows joining since the newest download are new places; no existing row joins or leaves. Since the tag,
all 970 additions join, plus Torres Strait Island (7548), reclassified from `region` to `municipality`;
13 Mexican municipio rows leave, including Tototlán. That older Australian join and those Mexican departures already
shipped in v6.2.0-export.1.

The filter includes the new Federal District regions, Spanish municipios, Italian `ex comune` territories and
`section` rows. It is a starting selection, not a guarantee that every result is an independent settlement.
TYPE_FIELD.md now explains that a section's population is part of its city's, never extra. To sum or rank populations,
leave out sections and choose one scope: in Spain count municipio rows, not their main villages as well. Puigcerdà's
municipio has 10,035 people and its main village 7,909 of those same people. A name search can return both.

## Known limits

- Brazil: ten recall places remain unproved. São Bento (PB), Santa Rita, Morros and Bom Jardim (MA), Barro (CE),
  Vera Cruz and Terra Nova (BA), Terra Roxa (PR) and Costa Rica (MS) have no matching official locality in their
  containing município; Santa Teresa (Rio) has no proved open point inside its bairro. The recall seat rule and
  documented exceptions do not prove those places or improve the strict point-to-point score.
- Planaltina (13934) stays `city`, with its old population: its row has no own Wikidata item proving it is the
  administrative region. Sol Nascente/Pôr do Sol, Arniqueira and Água Quente lack contained open points;
  26 de Setembro and Ponte Alta have insufficient item and point evidence. Brasília continues to represent Plano Piloto.
- Rio also holds Ilha de Guaratiba and Barra Olímpica for missing own open points. Santa Teresa is held in both the
  register and recall lists, not an extra missing bairro. Anaurilândia's item hold is fixed here, but its population
  has not gained a year. Some other smaller Brazilian places keep old figures whose municipal origin was not proved.
- Spain's scope review retains 57 held cases: six unadded municipios (Ceuta, Los Guájares, Belver de Cinca,
  Sarriés/Sartze, Calvarrasa de Arriba and Villaescusa), and 51 existing rows with unproved identity or classification.
  Ten of those existing rows have only their wrong population cleared; 41 stay held without that repair. A point
  outside the municipio or conflicting item blocks an addition. Vallehermosa's older village-versus-municipio
  mismatch also remains for later review.
- Italian `ex comune` rows are historical territories included by the unchanged filter. Strict inhabited-place
  users should additionally exclude them. France and Italy still have unproved parent, item, recall or population
  cases; Romanian Oraș Mărășești and Iclănzel still lack a population proved to match today's membership.
- The limits listed in [v6.1.0](v6.1.0.md#known-limits) and [v6.2.0](v6.2.0.md#known-limits) remain unless fixed above.
  In particular, not every held US item was filled: the new township items resolve much of the old rate-limit
  backlog, but unproved items and duplicate candidates remain. Retained US territorial towns, Romanian comune and
  Polish gminy still limit settlement-only use; removals and incompatible query changes wait for v7.

## Upgrading

No query change is needed. Pin the export tag and its source commit, then refresh the data. New rows have new ids; no
id is removed. The export's migration archives list added ids, changed fields and identity decisions, both as a step
from v6.2.0-export.1 and cumulatively from the last major, v5.2.0; check their `from` and `to` commits and checksums.

[DATA_VERSIONING.md](https://github.com/dr5hn/countries-states-cities-database/blob/3f8e8be78aa4a36016479e3fd3566355e58215fa/DATA_VERSIONING.md)
now documents an existing export limitation: JSON exports, and the MongoDB dump built from them, write city and state
`level` and `parent_id` as strings, while source files and SQL use integers. Until v7, read non-null values as integers
and keep nulls as null. This release does not change that representation. Keep the source attribution notices with
redistributed data, and consult the repository-only migration supplements above when they apply to stored ids.
